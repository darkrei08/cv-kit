# CV Standards in Europe, 2026

This document records what is actually documented, in 2026, about CV conventions in eleven European markets plus the two EU-level instruments (Europass and EURES) that cross-border applications run through. It is for anyone preparing a CV for more than one country — a candidate, a coach, or a tool that generates CVs — and it separates what a national public employment service (PES) or an EU source actually says from what is only local folklore. Conventions here are practice, not law, unless a source says otherwise; the employer's advert and the PES instruction for that country always outrank this document. `[n]` markers resolve to full URLs in "Matrix sources" and "Other sources" at the end. `UNVERIFIED` means exactly that: no primary source was found, or a mark the underlying research brief already carried has been kept visible instead of being resolved by guessing.

### The three contradictions, left open

- **Germany, photo.** The Federal Employment Agency page says a photo is no longer a must and, on the same page, tells candidates that a Bewerbungsfoto makes a good impression and should come from a studio photographer [1]. Do not turn either half into a rule: hold the contradiction and let the advert decide.
- **Netherlands, photo.** werk.nl's advice page tells candidates to always add a photo [6]; that is advice from a PES, not a statutory rule, and other sources treat the photo as optional. On top of that, UWV stopped displaying personal data on CVs held on werk.nl after one account viewed some 150,000 CVs; a phone number stays visible only if the candidate allowed phone contact [7]. Follow the specific employer's or PES instruction per application.
- **Nordics, photo and civil status.** No explicit official national statement requiring omission was found; "not expected" is a convention [14], and it stays UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Europass today

Europass is a free set of online tools and information developed by the European Commission: a profile, a CV and cover-letter editor, a document library, skills and interests sections, and job and course search [19]. It is open to anyone regardless of nationality or country of residence [18].

What it is **not**:

- Not a certification. "Using Europass does not entitle you to any automatic recognition of your skills and qualifications or other entitlements" [18].
- Not a translator. "Europass does not translate the information you have already provided in your profile or documents" [19]. A CV in a second language is content you must write, not a template you can switch on.
- Not a place for sensitive data. The FAQ tells users never to include health, beliefs or career-irrelevant information in a profile, and never to store documents containing sensitive personal information in the library [19].

Its language counts are inconsistent inside its own pages: the FAQ says the profile, CV and cover letter can be created in 29 languages [19], the same FAQ elsewhere says a profile can be created in 30 languages [19], and the "Create your Europass CV" page says 31 [20]. The brief records the same spread (29/30/31) and asks for the FAQ values as working numbers without asserting a universal 31. Treat the exact count as UNVERIFIED.

**Export parsing is unresolved.** No EU source was found establishing that a Europass export parses safely in applicant tracking systems, and none establishing that it is rejected. Third-party tests contradict each other: one reports field-level parse failures on real Europass CVs [25], another reports clean machine-readable text whose only real limitation is a fixed section order [26]. Both are commercially interested, low-authority sources. The defensible position: describe Europass as neither guaranteed safe nor rejected, and settle the question on the actual parser named by the actual employer, using the actual exported file.

## 3. EURES since April 2025

EURES jobseeker accounts, CVs included, were closed on 31 March 2025, and Europass became the platform for jobseekers and learners from 1 April 2025 [21]. The network itself did not move: EURES keeps its CV and vacancy database and serves jobseekers in the 27 EU countries plus Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland, while employers and EURES advisers can still see candidate profiles transferred to Europass and shared with EURES [21]. CVs had to be exported before 31 March 2025; after that the profile must be recreated in Europass, and sharing with EURES is switched on from the Europass profile [21] — a flow the Commission has since simplified [24].

- **Consent is the legal basis.** EURES states that personal data is processed because, under Article 5(1)(d) of Regulation (EU) 2018/1725, the user has given consent to processing for one or more specific purposes, and that consent can be withdrawn at any time [22].
- **Structured fields are the point of the platform, but optional in the terms.** The EURES data protection statement lists the CV/jobseeker profile fields — education and professional background, language and personal skills, desired location, desired occupation — and the desired-employment fields (preferred occupation, job title, position, location, contract type and duration) as non-mandatory [22]. Completing them is what makes matching and adviser search work; it is not a legal obligation.
- **Do not over-supply.** Date of birth, nationality and gender are optional account fields in the same statement [22].
- EURES also states that personal data will not be used for automated decision-making, including profiling [22].

Practical reading: maintain the Europass profile, complete the structured fields for education, experience, skills, desired location and occupation, connect it to EURES, and record the consent given — and nothing else. Certificates need not be attached unless the advert asks or matching requires them.

## 4. Do ESCO labels belong on a CV?

ESCO is the EU's multilingual classification of skills, competences, qualifications and occupations — a dictionary that identifies and classifies occupations and skills for the EU labour market [17]. Europass uses it to give users a pre-defined list of occupations to choose from in the work-experience section, and to show full occupation descriptions on demand [17].

- **Labels, yes; identifiers, no.** ESCO concepts are identified by URIs meant for data exchange — for example `http://data.europa.eu/esco/occupation/528f90ed-e250-48bd-aacc-ffb7b1de5654` identifies the occupation "Textile specialised seller" [23]. A URI or a numeric code on a CV is a machine identifier, not a job title. Use the occupation and skill wording as ordinary text.
- **Use the wording in the reader's language.** ESCO labels exist in many languages and Europass serves the occupation list in the interface language [17]; the label is the human-readable name, so the CV should carry the name and never the identifier.
- **Where labels earn their place.** In the Europass profile they are how the occupation is stated for EURES matching; in a plain CV they are most useful as the exact wording a vacancy uses, provided that wording is true of the candidate.
- **Boundary.** No EU source was found that instructs candidates to print ESCO codes, or that forbids it; that prohibition is a project rule and stays UNVERIFIED as an EU rule. The mechanical reason stands on its own: codes and URIs are noise in a document read by people and by parsers that do not consume ESCO identifiers.

## 5. Languages

| Entry | Defensible? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Native language, stated as native | Yes | A fact the candidate can assert. |
| Foreign language with a CEFR level (A1–C2) | Yes, if evidenced or honestly self-assessed | The CEFR scale is the expected way to state language ability in at least one PES market, Austria [3]; "defensible" still means the candidate would sign it. |
| A foreign language level inferred from the fact that a CV exists in that language | No | Europass explicitly does not translate entered content [19]: writing an English CV demonstrates effort, not proficiency. |
| "English: excellent" with no level and no basis | No | Unsupported superlative; the level vocabulary exists precisely to avoid it. |
| A language studied but not used, listed without a level | No | It adds nothing and invites a question the candidate cannot answer. |

Rule of thumb: a language line is defensible when the candidate can hold a short interview in that language and can produce evidence or a self-assessment they would sign. Everything else stays off the CV until it can be tested.
