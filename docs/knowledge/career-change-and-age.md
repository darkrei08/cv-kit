# A long career, and the age question

Most CV advice is written for someone with five years of experience and one
employer. This document is about the other case: a career of thirty or forty years
in manual and technical work, assembled from fixed-term contracts, with a labour
record an employer can request from a public employment service.

It applies to any experienced worker in a technical trade. The rules are about
structure and honesty, not about a country.

## What a trade employer screens for

A hiring manager for a workshop, a quarry, a site or a warehouse reads a CV
differently from an office recruiter. In order of attention:

1. **Can this person do the job on day one?** Machine types, licences, safety
   authorisations, the specific processes actually performed.
2. **Is the work record real?** Dates that add up, employers that exist, a record
   that matches what a reference call will say.
3. **Will they turn up, and will they stay?** Continuity, repeated contracts with the
   same employer, references who answer the phone.

Nothing else moves the decision much. Awards, personal statements and hobbies are
decoration on the first screen. What they are checking is competence and reliability,
and both are evidenced by dates and machines.

The practical consequence: **the dates are the main argument, not the duties**. A
duty list describes what anyone in the role does; a date range describes what this
person actually did, and for how long.

## Presenting a career built from short contracts

Many short contracts look like instability until they are grouped. Three moves fix
the reading:

**Group by employer, not by contract.** Eight fixed-term contracts with the same
company over five years is one block: *"2014–2018, Company, addetto impianti —
contracts renewed several times."* The reader now sees five years of loyalty, not
eight job changes.

**Name the reason for a move when the reason is neutral.** "Contract ended, staff
reduction, contract completed" removes the question a reader would otherwise ask.
A missing reason reads as concealment; a mundane reason reads as ordinary work.

**State fixed-term and part-time honestly, in the date line.** Seasonal work, job on
call, vouchers: hidden they look like a gap; declared they look like a market reality.

**Compress beyond fifteen years, expand the last ten.** The 1979-1990 block can be
three lines naming employers, trades and date ranges. The last ten years should carry
three to five bullets each, with machines and processes named.

## What to do about age

Age discrimination in hiring is documented by field experiments and official reports;
it is not a suspicion. It is also illegal in access to employment in the European
Union, which changes nothing about whether it happens.

The document-level countermeasures that actually do something:

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Lead with the specific, verifiable specialism | A reader comparing "17 years of blacksmith work" with "knows the trade" is comparing evidence with a claim |
| Keep the last ten years detailed and current | Recent competence is the strongest available signal; it is also the only part of a long career an employer can check quickly |
| Omit the graduation year of a school qualification | It is an age cue with no selection value. Keep the year of recent professional training: that works in your favour |
| Avoid the formula "N years of experience" in the profile | It announces age before competence, and it is a claim rather than a fact. Use the domain-specific periods instead: "17 consecutive years with one employer" |
| Do not list age, date of birth, marital status or children in a document that goes to a parser | Unnecessary personal data and a known discrimination vector. In a hand-delivered CV for recipients who still expect it, that is a judgement call — make it deliberately |
| Put availability, shift tolerance and travel in writing | Counteracts the assumption that an experienced worker will refuse nights or travel |
| Keep the technical vocabulary current | Obsolete terminology reads as "has not worked recently" more strongly than any date |

Two honest limits must be stated, because they are the reason these measures are worth
doing at all and not a miracle:

- They work at the margins. A reader who has decided to filter by age will filter by
  age, and the dates reveal it anyway.
- Hiding dates — the advice found in some guides — is worse than the problem. It reads
  as evasion, breaks the reverse-chronology convention every recruiter expects, and
  fails the first reference check.

## Licences and certificates: how to present them honestly

**A licence held but not practised.** State possession, then state the experience
factually: *"Categories C and D held; driving experience gained with trucks and
earth-moving equipment in quarries and construction sites, not with passenger-carrying
vehicles."* This is better than both alternatives — silence about the licence loses the
screening keyword, and silence about the gap invites a claim the candidate cannot
support in the first week. It also removes the self-disqualifying note common in
older CVs ("I never had the chance to practise"), which damages a profile for no
benefit.

**Certificates whose refresh cycle has passed.** An attestation is evidence that
training happened, not that a qualification is current. Show the date of the course
and never claim validity that has not been checked on the document. Refresh cycles
differ by subject and by the rules in force when the course was taken:

| Subject | Cycle to verify | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Workplace first aid | 3 years (Italian rule) | D.M. 388/2003 |
| Fire-fighting officer | Periodic, by risk level; the 2021 decrees replaced the 1998 rules | D.M. 2 September 2021 |
| General and specific worker safety training | 6 hours every 5 years | State–Regions agreement, 17 April 2025 |
| Forklift authorisation | Periodic renewal, with a practical component | current State–Regions agreement |
| Professional driver qualification (CQC) | Periodic course; a long expiry triggers a revision exam | MIT / national driving portal |
| Welder qualification (ISO 9606-1) | By process and range, not automatically three years | certification body scheme |

Mark every row `UNVERIFIED` until the certificate itself has been read. A refresh
interval quoted from memory is exactly the kind of claim that collapses in an interview.

## Wording: what helps and what disqualifies

| Wording | Effect |
|---|---|
| "17 consecutive years with the same employer" | Verifiable continuity; the single strongest line an experienced worker can write |
| "Available for night shifts, weekends and travel" | Removes the main objection to an older candidate |
| "Repaired the crushing plant directly on site, reducing external assistance" | Names a cost the employer saves |
| "Over 40 years of experience" | Age first, evidence never |
| "Hard worker, dynamic, reliable" | Adjectives with no content, discarded on sight |
| "I am available for any task" | Reads as no specialism, and invites the least attractive role |
| "I have not had the chance to practise category D" | Volunteered self-disqualification |

The rule behind the table: **a claim that cannot be checked is worth less than a fact
that can**, and a fact that reveals a limitation is worth more than an omission that
looks like concealment.

## A note on the record itself

In several European countries a public employment service can issue a work-history
record listing employers, dates, contract types and occupational classifications. Where
that exists, it settles arguments — and it means a CV that disagrees with it is not
merely inaccurate but risky. Build the CV from that record, and keep the differences
between the CV and any earlier version written down where the candidate can see them.
